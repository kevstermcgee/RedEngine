# 2026-10-05. Analysis and props share the ground the player walks on
Status: accepted
Summary: One blocker query on MapWorld feeds reach, routes, lint and diagnostics; unreachable points say what covers them; loose props rest on the real ground (a heightfield) instead of a flat floor.

## Context
In an endless generated world (`procgen`) the trees and shrubs that stop the player are in no authored collider list. The player's movement asks the generator for them each tick, but reachability, route planning, `lint` and the diagnostics asked only the authored list: a trunk read as open ground, `lint` reported a perimeter "leak" in a world with no perimeter (on Marcel and the endless example), `plan` drew one floor height of a world with a hundred, and an unreachable point said only "NOT reachable". A fresh-author exercise (`examples/lantern_walk`) then found the same assumption inside the game itself: `PropWorld` put "a floor under everything" at a flat `y = 0` slab, so a loose prop in a hollow lay under it (the pick-up ray was "blocked by something solid"), a dropped prop on a hill sank into the slope, and the scripted `interact` stopped at a horizontal distance that ignored how far below the eye a prop on a slope is.

## Decision
`MapWorld::is_endless`, `blockers_in(min, max)` (the authored colliders plus `ProcgenGround::colliders_near`, the call the player's own movement makes; borrowed and free without a generated world) and `analysis_box` are the one shared query. `reach::compute` floods a window (48 m round the start, widened to hold the targets, 0.25 m cells) instead of the solid bounds, reports no leak at the window's edge, and `lint` treats a generated world as open. Planned routes end on the exact goal when the real physics can walk it. `pathing::unreachable_why` says what covers an unreachable point (a generated tree or shrub with its place and size, or the authored object) and the nearest standable spot with its height; `verify`'s reach rows and `reach --to` use it. A zone authored at the wrong height names the ground height. `plan` draws every standable height and the generated blockers of an endless world. Loose props are excluded from the headroom rule (they are carried, not ceilings). `PropWorld` builds, when the ground is not flat (a `terrain` or `procgen`) and loose props exist, a heightfield sampled from the same ground function the player stands on (`GroundCandidates::terrain_height_at`) under the props with 40 m of margin and a deep catch-floor below; flat scenes keep their flat floor unchanged. The grid is offset by irregular fractions of a cell because parry's heightfield ray cast misses a ray running exactly along a grid line (measured) and the margin would otherwise put every prop's own coordinates on a line. `interact` walks until the eye-to-prop distance is within 80% of the reach (`approach::pickup_within`, never looser than before). Visual-only vegetation (grass, flowers) is not a blocker and is not in the query. Abstract reachability and physical replay stay separate questions; the tests ask both on the same scene and expect agreement.

## Consequences
Props now work on generated and heightfield ground; a prop thrown beyond the 40 m margin falls to the catch-floor, and a looping (`world.wrap`) axis is not folded into the heightfield. Analysis of an endless world covers a window, not infinity: a target beyond it is reachable only if the caller includes it. What still disagrees: `ray`/`sight` and the `nav` graph ignore generated trees (a tree may block sight in play and not in `ray`), and `plan --ascii` is still one floor.

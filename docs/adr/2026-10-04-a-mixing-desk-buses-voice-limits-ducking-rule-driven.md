# 2026-10-04. A mixing desk: buses, voice limits, ducking, rule-driven layers and checks.audio
Status: accepted
Summary: Sounds go through buses with levels, a per-bus voice limit that steals the least important sound, thinning of bursts and ducking of the music; a scene can add a room, duck on events and fade scores with rule variables; checks.audio holds a scene's sound to a standard in verify.

## Context
Audio plan phases 4 and 5. Until now every sound was fire-and-forget: forty guns at once all played, a flurry of footsteps was a wall of identical clicks, nothing could be cut off, the music never made room for an explosion, the ambience and the player's switches were separate flags, and a scene had no way to say what its sound should be like, or to be checked for it.

## Decision
- **`mixer`** (pure, tested without a sound card): `Bus` (music, sfx, ui, ambience) under a master with `Levels` (the player's switches are levels of 0). `Mixer::request(now, cue)` answers whether a sound may start and at what level: a muted bus plays nothing; at most 3 starts of the same sound (`Cue::key`) within 60 ms, which thins a burst; each bus has a voice limit (sfx 24, ambience 6, ui 4, music 8) and when it is full the least important voice (lowest `Priority`, then quietest, then nearest its end) is stolen if the newcomer is more important or clearly louder (25%), else the newcomer is dropped, so atmosphere never evicts gameplay. A cue may carry a `Duck`: the music falls by `depth` in `attack` seconds, stays down while the sound plays, and returns over `release`. `wet` puts a dry clip in a room (the engine's reverb) and returns it with its tail. `hear(listeners, source)` is what several listeners hear (the loudest listener's gain and pan, so a shot beside player two is loud for the room, not four times as loud): split-screen will use it.
- **`Audio`** (the rodio player) runs every sound through the mixer: sounds are tracked voices that can be stolen, the old music loop and the scene's music layers sit under the duck, `play` and `play_at` keep their levels and go through the sfx bus.
- **Scene `audio`** gains `reverb` (a room for the nature calls), `duck` (events from the rules that pull the music down) and `layers` (a score that fades in while a rule variable is at least a value: intensity that follows the game). The state dump reports layer levels and the number of ducks, so a headless script can assert them (`tests/client_headless.rs` does).
- **`checks.audio`** in a scene, run by `verify` with everything else: `scores` hold every score the scene names to the loop standard, a loudness window and a peak ceiling; `sounds` hold any built-in or file to LUFS, peak, seam and length; `soundscape` runs the ambience state machine in a place and hour and asserts the calls heard and the bed gains settled at ("owls call at night and never at noon"). Marcel carries eight of them.

## Consequences
Easier: a game gets a sound policy without writing one; mistakes in a scene's sound (a score too loud, an owl at noon) fail `verify`. Harder: the voice limit is a policy that can drop a quiet sound that the player might have wanted; a scene-level ducking only reacts to events the rules raise; reverb on calls is baked per clip (cached), not a live send; and the existing kart, shooter and menu sounds are not yet given priorities or keys of their own (they all play as normal-priority sfx, thinned only by identical-clip bursts). Per-player listeners are built and tested (`hear`) but wired in by split-screen.

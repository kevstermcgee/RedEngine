# 2026-10-04. A living soundscape: nature sounds, birdsong by the hour, and music by the sun
Status: accepted
Summary: The countryside is synthesised (wind, leaves, crickets, bees, seven bird calls), a pure state machine decides what is heard from the sun's height, the place and the walker, and a scene audio block adds a score per time of day crossfaded by the sun.

## Context
Marcel is a world you walk through for hours, and what makes a field feel alive is mostly what you hear: birds at first light, wind that gusts, leaves, crickets once the sun is down, an owl. The audio work so far (voices, scores, loop-safe rendering, measurement) could make any sound but had no nature sounds, no way to schedule a bird at a random moment, and no runtime that changes sound with the time of day. The player only had a single music loop.

## Decision
- **`nature`** synthesises the countryside from noise and sines: five seamless stereo **beds** (wind with gusts, rustling leaves, crickets, the still air of night, bees) rendered at a stated loudness, and seven bird **calls** (robin, blackbird, wren, chaffinch, cuckoo, pigeon, tawny owl) whose phrases differ by seed. They are listed in `audio list` (`ambience.nature.*`, `bird.*`) and held to the loop and one-shot standards, so their loudness, seams, brightness and spectrogram are measured, not guessed (a test also checks that wind is low and breathy, leaves bright, crickets near 4.5 kHz, an owl below 700 Hz, a wren above 3.5 kHz).
- **`ambience`** is a pure state machine: told the sun's height, whether it is morning, the kind of country and the listener's speed, it returns a gain per bed, a weight per mood of music (always summing to 1, continuous as the sun goes round) and the calls that start now, from a seeded Poisson process whose rates follow the real day: a dawn chorus from before sunrise to a couple of hours after (robins, blackbirds, wrens, chaffinches), scattered song through the day with the odd cuckoo and pigeon, a gentler dusk song, silence at night broken by an owl; crickets at night, bees over flowers at noon, leaves in woods, more wind in fields; running hushes the birds. Tests simulate minutes of each hour and assert these (the dawn chorus has at least four times the calls of noon, owls only at night, no birds at night).
- **The `audio` scene block** (`ambience: "nature"`, a score file per mood, `music_volume`, `ambience_volume`) turns it on. The live client renders the loops on a worker thread, adds each as a silent looping layer and sets volumes every frame (eased, so a toggle is a fade); music follows the player's `music` setting (default on, N and the pause menu's button), ambience their `sound` setting. A headless run (no sound card) still steps the state machine and writes it to the state dump (`/audio/{beds,music,calls,recent}`), so a script can say `expect /audio/calls/robin min 5`.

## Consequences
Easier: a game with a clock and a `procgen` world gets a day's worth of sound with five lines of JSON; the music is the game's own scores (Marcel's four live in `examples/marcel/audio`). Harder: calls are plain synthesis, not recordings, so they suggest birds rather than fool a birdwatcher; every sound is mono and panned, not positioned in space (no distance filtering yet); rendering four scores takes seconds in a debug build (a worker does it while the game starts); and the mixer is volumes on looping sinks, not a bus with ducking or voice limits (the audio plan's phase 4).

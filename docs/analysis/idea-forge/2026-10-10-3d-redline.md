# Engine feedback from a hand-directed build: Redline (2026-10-10)

- Not an Idea Forge run: Kevin asked for an hours-long 3D game; a Claude session designed and built it and published it to RedEngineGames
  (`projects/redline`, releases `redline-v1` to `redline-v3`). It uses this folder's format so that its findings join the backlog under
  the same keys as the Idea Forge runs.
- Engine revision: `9c62ea7b3b03` (no engine changes; re-checked unchanged on `384d1b0`, which touched only docs and examples); kind 3D; slug `redline`.
- The long-form write-up with code locations and repros is `docs/analysis/2026-10-10-what-building-redline-taught-us-about-red.md`.

## The game

**Mechanic:** Quake-style momentum platforming (bunny hop, air strafing, 9.5 m/s base, max 22 m/s) through 19 short chambers over lava,
against a run clock that only grows when you vent a chamber. Beat a chamber's par for a streak.
Loop: a hub (the Foundry) with 7 persisted upgrades, 6 difficulty Heats, a relic shrine and records; dive, chain chambers drawn at random
by tier (no repeats in a run), burn out, spend sparks, dive again. One generated scene: 2,525 objects, 657 rules, 227 vars (44
persisted), 86 zones, 25 `checks.sim` scenarios (every chamber cleared at base speed by a scripted runner, plus the run loop), three
synthesized music layers. After Kevin's first look: first-person view made explicit, the player is the `robot` body, and a minimal HUD.

## Idea fit

What the design wanted and the engine could not express, in the order a player would notice it:
- **Sound for the game's own events** (F2): pickups, checkpoints, an under-par vent and a burn are silent; this is the biggest gap
  between a data-only game and one that feels good to play.
- **Portals that keep your speed** (F8): a momentum game wants a vent to carry the run into the next chamber; teleport stops the player.
- **A minimal, content-sized HUD** (F5): the owner asked for one; the layout's fixed widths and the panel's jump allow only fewer rows.
- **A player recoloured to the world** (F14), **a restart from the pause menu** (F15), and **readable randomness** (F10).

## What worked

The engine carried a full roguelite in data with no code: persisted progress, start and end cards with restart, var-driven music
layers, per-scene momentum tuning, and validation that named every mistake. `sim` was fast and deterministic enough to prove all 19
chambers clearable on every commit, and the headless client's dump found a real game bug (a vent's banner overwritten in the same tick)
that no screenshot would have shown.

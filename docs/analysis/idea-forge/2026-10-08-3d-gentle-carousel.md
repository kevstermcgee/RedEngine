## The game

**Mechanic: you chalk every tile you step on (it lights a lamp and crumbles a few seconds later) but may only step while a partner you do not control, the blue moth, is fluttering.** Core loop: on a 5x5 tile courtyard over a dark floor, look at the board showing a house, then walk a connected path of tiles that traces it. The moth flutters 4 s and rests 2 s, forever; during its rest you must stand still, because stepping onto a fresh tile startles the shy dark (a fright and you are sent home). Tiles ahead are revealed as you reach their neighbours (the map builds one step ahead) and chalked tiles age one tide and break the next (4 s tide, so 4-8 s of life), so stopping in the wrong place drops you. Win: light all 12 lamps of the house with at most 2 smudges (off-picture tiles). Lose: 3 frights, 3 smudges, or falling when your tile crumbles. Adapted: the picture is shown on a board and the lit lamps float above the tiles, so the path's picture survives after the ground decays; the partner is a prop on its own island that jumps along a line, not a free-roaming creature. Cut: no per-tile chalk decals, no real 'frozen' movement (a penalty replaces it), 2 or more rooms.

## Idea fit

Expressed: chalk-as-state (per-tile var, show/hide lamps), the map building ahead (activate on enter) and decaying behind (deactivate on a tide), the picture goal (counters), win and loses, all as data with six sim scenarios. Not expressible: 'you can only move while the partner moves' (no action can lock or slow the player; replaced by a penalty), per-object timers (a global tide and generated per-tile rules), and a real void (the implicit ground makes falling a low-zone trick). A first-person player does all of it with their feet; the partner is only a clock and a moving prop, so the BUT is felt as a rhythm, not as a partner. Not tried: playing it by hand in a window.
